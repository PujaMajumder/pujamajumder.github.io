## About
How do bacteria detect phage infection and stop it from spreading? I study the molecular mechanisms that make bacterial immunity work.

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Bioscience and Biotechnology at IIT Kharagpur and lead the Majumder Lab. We combine structural biology, biophysics, biochemistry, microbiology, and computational biology to discover bacterial antiphage defense systems and explain how they function.

My research connects immune signals to three kinds of cellular responses: opening membrane pores, remodeling cellular nucleotides, and assembling protein filaments that deplete metabolites to restrict phage replication. Through cryo-EM, X-ray crystallography, and functional experiments, we link molecular structures to the defense strategies they enable. Computational biology helps us identify new systems and understand their functional mechanisms.

My work includes studies of the CRISPR-associated effectors Cat1, Cad1, and Cam1, published in Science, Cell, and Nature, alongside research on membrane transporters and antibacterial recognition and efflux. Before joining IIT Kharagpur, I worked at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center and trained at the Indian Institute of Science.

I welcome conversations with researchers interested in bacterial immunity, structural biology, and collaborative approaches to molecular mechanism, as well as prospective students and postdoctoral researchers interested in the lab.

Explore our research and opportunities: https://pujamajumder.github.io/
