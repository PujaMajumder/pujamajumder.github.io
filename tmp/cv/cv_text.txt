Research focus

I investigate how bacterial antiphage defense systems translate immune signals into molecular responses that restrict viral replication. My lab combines cryo-EM, X-ray crystallography, biophysics, biochemistry, microbiology, and computational biology to connect protein structure with immune function.

Contributions to science

CRISPR membrane pores and lipid gating

Membrane-associated immune effectors link intracellular infection signals to growth arrest, but the structural basis of their activation requires direct mechanistic analysis. Cap1 and Chp1 reveal distinct routes from cyclic oligoadenylate recognition to membrane depolarization. Cap1 opens a preformed tetrameric pore following cyclic tetra-adenylate binding. Chp1 undergoes a lipid-gated conformational switch that removes pore obstruction and permits ion flow. These studies connect second-messenger signalling to antiviral growth arrest. Cap1: co-first author; accepted in Nature Structural & Molecular Biology. Chp1: first and co-corresponding author, bioRxiv 2026.

Filament assembly and NAD depletion

TIR-domain immune proteins can couple higher-order assembly to catalytic activity. I resolved the active supramolecular assembly of the Cat1 CARF-TIR effector by cryo-EM. Structural and functional studies linked cyclic signal recognition, filament-network formation, and a composite NADase active site to depletion of cellular NAD during antiviral defence. Co-first and co-corresponding author, Science 2025.

Metabolic reprogramming in antiviral defence

Type III CRISPR-Cas effectors can defend cells by changing essential metabolite pools. I combined X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, and biochemical characterization to define signal recognition and catalytic regulation in Cad1. The work established how a cyclic nucleotide-activated adenosine deaminase converts ATP to ITP, arrests infected-cell growth, and limits phage propagation. Co-first author, Cell 2024.

Antibody-assisted structural biology of membrane effectors

I used high-affinity antibody fragments as fiducial markers to resolve the small membrane-associated CRISPR effector Cam1. Its tetrameric architecture provides a structural basis for understanding membrane depolarization during type III CRISPR immunity. Nature 2024.

Antibacterial transport and allosteric regulation

Multidrug efflux pumps protect bacteria from chemically diverse antibacterial compounds and contribute to antimicrobial resistance. I determined the nanobody-bound QacA transporter structure at 3.6 angstrom resolution and used structure-guided functional assays to establish the role of an extracellular helical hairpin in efflux. Earlier work identified acidic residues governing substrate recognition, proton coupling, and multidrug transport. First-author papers in The EMBO Journal 2023 and Journal of Molecular Biology 2019.
